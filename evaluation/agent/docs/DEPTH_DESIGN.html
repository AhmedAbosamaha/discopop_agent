Why the two setups differ. A rewrite without a pragma counts as having made nothing parallel yet, so the loop around it and the function keep their full share of the time and are offered again. A rewrite that carries the model's pragmas counts as parallel, and what is around it is offered again only where 1 % of the time or more is still sequential.

Two things in the agent that would blur a depth experiment

  1. The first round changes too. Today the agent sends a rewrite back when the program is not faster than before, and the model tries again. With a second round to come, the agent no longer does that in the first round: it keeps every safe rewrite and asks about speed only at the end. That was your decision of 26 Sep, for a good reason then — a later rewrite may be what makes an earlier one pay. But in our saved trials this “not faster, try again” fired in 38 of 90 trials, and 25 of those ended faster. So “depth 0 against depth 1” would differ in two things, not one.
  2. A second rewrite can take the credit of the first. A rewrite is kept if the program with DiscoPoP's pragmas on the loops near the changed lines is not slower than the program before it — and that program has no pragmas yet. If the second rewrite touches the loops the first one made parallel, it is timed as if it had made them parallel itself. A second rewrite that adds nothing, or loses part of the gain, would be kept. The final check against the original program still holds, so no wrong result would be reported; but “what the second round gained” could not be read off.

Both are read in the agent's code. Neither has been shown by a run.
